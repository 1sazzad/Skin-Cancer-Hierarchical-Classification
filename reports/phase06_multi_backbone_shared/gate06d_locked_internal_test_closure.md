## Primary scientific findings

1. Hard hierarchical routing underperformed matched flat classification for six of seven backbones.
2. MobileNetV3-Large was the only backbone with a numerically positive shared-minus-flat macro-F1 difference (+0.017968), but its paired-bootstrap 95% CI crossed zero and McNemar's exact test was non-significant. Therefore the result must not be described as statistically established superiority.
3. Oracle routing improved macro-F1 substantially over predicted routing for every backbone. This shows that upstream routing errors consistently suppress downstream predictive capability across architectures.
4. The flat-vs-hierarchical penalty is therefore not strictly backbone-independent in magnitude or sign, but routing remains a cross-backbone bottleneck.
5. EfficientNet-B2 illustrates that macro-F1 bootstrap inference and McNemar correctness inference can disagree because they test different quantities; this is not a contradiction.

## Locked interpretation

The supported conclusion is:

> Hard hierarchical routing generally reduces end-to-end performance relative to matched flat classification across the tested backbone family, although the magnitude and even direction of the effect are architecture-dependent. Across all seven backbones, oracle routing substantially outperforms predicted routing, indicating that upstream routing errors are a persistent source of lost downstream predictive capability.

The following stronger statements are not supported:

- hierarchy is always inferior to flat classification;
- MobileNetV3-Large hierarchy is statistically superior to its flat counterpart;
- all architecture effects are significant under every paired test;
- oracle performance is a deployable model result.
